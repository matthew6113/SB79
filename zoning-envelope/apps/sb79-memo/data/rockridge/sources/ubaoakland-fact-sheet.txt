5727 College Avenue Project: What You Need to Know
Two market-rate senior-living towers, 26 and 23 stories, proposed for the Trader Joe's site at 5727 College Avenue, with no affordable units and a grocery space that carries no commitment to a grocer.
Take action: sign the petition to save Trader Joe's and stop the Two Towers.
Sign the petition →
Status Formal application submitted 8/19/2026 (Planning No. ZW2601986); SB 330 vesting preliminary application ZP260014 submitted 4/22/2026
Location 5727 College Avenue (the current Trader Joe's site)
Developer Align Real Estate / Albertsons (Rockridge Property Owners, LLC)
Height 306 ft (26 stories) and 276 ft (23 stories), atop a 40-ft base building
Units 372 units total (senior living / continuing care)
Affordable None (no dedicated affordable units)
The proposed development at 5727 College Avenue (the current Trader Joe’s site) has raised
significant community questions regarding its size, density, and alignment with local
zoning, as well as the future of Trader Joe’s and of publicly accessible commercial
frontage on College Avenue. This informational overview breaks down how state regulations
and local codes are shaping the project’s trajectory, as well as limitations on the city’s
ability to modify/reject the project.
Updated August 19, 2026. Align filed a formal application on that date that reduces the
project from 31 and 25 stories to 26 and 23, dropping the towers from 352 and 294 feet to
306 and 276 feet. It also cuts 43 units and adds a grocery-sized retail space. See
What the August 2026 revision changed below for
what the revision does and does not do.
The Proposed Project at a Glance
Developers: Align Real Estate
Formal Application Date: 8/19/2026 (Planning No. ZW2601986)
Preliminary Application Date: 4/22/2026 (SB 330 vesting preliminary application ZP260014)
Project Type: Senior Living Facility (Continuing Care Retirement Community)
Proposed Height:
Tower One - 306 feet tall, 26 stories
Tower Two - 276 feet tall, 23 stories
Both towers sit atop a 40-foot base building
The site’s zoned height limit is 95 feet, roughly 8 stories. Tower One is more than three times that height, and about 18 stories above what the zoning allows without waivers.
Site Footprint: Covers nearly the entire 67,201 sq. ft. lot, with a 10-foot rear setback and 0-foot front and side setbacks, and only private open space atop the base building
Parking: 263 off-street residential spaces, 65 retail spaces, and 40 bicycle spaces
The existing Trader Joe’s has 82 parking spaces and is routinely full.
Residential parking entrances on both Miles and Oak Grove
3 off-street loading berths, with both loading entrances on Oak Grove
Affordable Units: Contains no dedicated affordable housing units
Retail/Commercial: 12,940 sq. ft. of retail with its entrance on Miles Avenue, identified as grocery space, plus 5,890 sq. ft. of other retail fronting College Avenue. No grocery operator is named, and no lease or commitment to Trader Joe’s, or to any grocer, is part of the application.
Total Project: 372 units, broken down as approximately 340 independent living, 12 assisted living, and 20 memory care. The assisted living units are placed in one tower and the memory care units in the other.
What the August 2026 Revision Changed
Align’s formal application, filed August 19, 2026, is the first revision since the April
preliminary application. Here is what moved and what did not.
What was reduced:
Original (April 2026) Revised (August 2026)
Tower One 31 stories / 352 ft 26 stories / 306 ft
Tower Two 25 stories / 294 ft 23 stories / 276 ft
Total units 415 372
Grocery space None 12,940 sq. ft.
The 43-unit reduction breaks down as 31 independent living units, 6 assisted living units,
and 6 memory care units.
Why the reduction stops where it does. Under SB 330, a preliminary application locks in
the zoning rules, policies, and fees in effect on the date it was filed. That lock is lost
if the developer later changes the unit count or square footage by 20 percent or more
( Gov. Code § 65941.1(d) ).
Cutting 43 of 415 units is a reduction of 10.36 percent, comfortably inside that 20 percent
limit. Align’s cover letter states that the revised project retains the rules in effect as of
its original April 2026 filing date.
What the revision does not do:
It does not add affordable housing. The revised project still contains zero dedicated affordable units.
It does not commit to a grocery store. The application sets aside space sized for a grocer. It does not name one, and it does not guarantee the store will be a Trader Joe’s rather than a higher-priced operator.
It does not cap the height. Align’s cover letter expressly reserves the right to build higher than the revised proposal. It describes the letter as an intent to apply for certain State Density Bonus Law rights “and to reserve the right to apply for others at a later date.”
It does not close off further waivers. The cover letter also reserves the right to request additional waivers, including waivers from objective design standards. That could, among other things, remove the requirement for commercial frontage on College Avenue.
It does not bring the project near the zoned height. At 306 and 276 feet, both towers remain roughly three times the 95-foot height area that applies to this site.
Key Laws & Regulatory Framework
The developers are navigating a complex intersection of California state housing mandates
and local city planning guidelines:
SB 330 (Housing Crisis Act of 2019): By submitting a “preliminary application,” the developers effectively froze the local zoning rules, policies, and fees in effect at that moment, preventing the city from changing requirements mid-project. That freeze survives a revision only if the unit count or square footage changes by less than 20 percent ( Gov. Code § 65941.1 ).
The Density Bonus Law & AB 2694: Expanded in 2024, state law now allows senior care facilities to claim density incentives (such as increased height and extra units) without being required to include affordable housing units. A qualifying “senior citizen housing development” receives a density bonus of 20 percent of the number of senior housing units ( Gov. Code § 65915(f)(3)(A) ).
Waivers of development standards: A developer may seek waivers of any standard that would physically preclude the project as designed. Unlike incentives and concessions, which are capped, the number of such waivers is not limited ( Gov. Code § 65915(e) ).
AB 130 (2025): Created a new statutory CEQA exemption for qualifying infill housing projects, codified at Pub. Resources Code § 21080.66 . The application filed for this site includes an AB 130 consistency analysis asserting the project’s eligibility for that exemption, which would remove environmental review from the process.
Height limits: Waived except if the waiver would result in a specific adverse impact on health or safety that cannot be mitigated or avoided
Off-street Parking Requirements: waived within ¼ mile of transit
Oakland General Plan Designation:
Zoning Use District: Neighborhood Commercial – CN-2 Zone
General Plan Designation: Mixed Housing Type Residential and Neighborhood Center Mixed Use
Height Area: 95 feet
The Legal Reality of Project Denial
Under the state’s Housing Accountability Act (HAA), the legal burden of proof required for
a city to deny or downsize an objective-compliant housing project is exceptionally high:
Specific, Adverse Impact: The city must prove by a preponderance of evidence that the project poses a significant, quantifiable, direct, and unavoidable hazard to public health or safety.
What doesn’t count: Vague arguments regarding “neighborhood character” or inconsistencies with local zoning ordinances do not legally qualify as health and safety impacts.
No Feasible Mitigation: A city cannot deny a project if there is any viable method to mitigate the safety risk other than reducing the project’s density or disapproving it entirely.
Financial Penalty Risks: If a municipality unlawfully denies an application under the HAA, it faces potential court orders to approve the project and mandatory fines starting at a minimum of $10,000 per housing unit.
See Gov. Code § 65589.5 for the full text of the Housing Accountability Act.
What UBA Would Like to See
UBA advocates for dense housing at this site up to eight stories, with appropriate setbacks
to help protect existing neighbors in single-family homes as well as apartment buildings.
The site should ideally have lots of housing for people of all income brackets. UBA has
always advocated for affordable housing in Rockridge, and this current proposal does nothing
to help close the gaping hole of affordability. We are opposed to high-rises in or adjacent
to Rockridge neighborhoods, but would welcome up to eight stories in areas zoned up to 95
feet. We welcome high-rises in large, undeveloped locations in or near Rockridge such as the
Ridge (Broadway and Pleasant Valley).
